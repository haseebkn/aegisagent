# Guardrail design for LLM-drafted STR narratives

## Summary

AegisAgent originally validated generated Suspicious Transaction Report narratives
by rejecting a blacklist of hedging words — *may, might, possibly, could, appears* —
and retrying until the model produced "definitive" prose. That control was wrong in
both directions. It has been replaced with a factual grounding check.

This note records why, because the reasoning matters more than the code.

---

## Why "zero hedging" is the wrong target

Section 7 of the PCMLTFA requires a reporting entity to report where it has
**reasonable grounds to suspect** that a transaction is related to a money
laundering or terrorist financing offence. Two features of that standard bear
directly on how a narrative should read:

**It is a suspicion standard, not a proof standard.** RGS sits below "reasonable
grounds to believe" and far below any evidentiary threshold. FINTRAC's own guidance
asks the reporting entity to describe the facts, context and ML/TF indicators that
*led to* the suspicion. The narrative's job is to let a FINTRAC analyst reconstruct
the reasoning — not to announce a conclusion.

**Overstatement is a defect.** A narrative asserting a transaction *is* laundering,
or that a cardholder *did* commit fraud, claims knowledge the reporting entity does
not have. It misrepresents the institution's actual epistemic position to a
regulator, and it is a poor foundation for anything downstream.

So an instruction to eliminate all hedging pushes the model in exactly the wrong
direction. It does not make the report more rigorous; it makes it less accurate
about how much is known. The right shape is: state observations plainly, then state
what those observations ground a suspicion of, and label which is which.

## Why a word filter does not work anyway

Beyond being aimed at the wrong property, the blacklist failed mechanically.

**It missed the failures that mattered.** Every narrative below passed the word
filter. All were generated by the pipeline and written to the compliance log:

> "This distance is physically impossible to traverse between the cardholder's last
> documented transaction location and the current merchant location."

The model was never given a prior transaction location. The premise is fabricated.

> "...representing a velocity spike of 67% above the 24-hour rate."

Invented arithmetic. It compares a 7-day count (25) against a 24-hour count (15) as
though they were the same quantity. They are not a rate relative to one another.

Neither statement contains a hedging word. Both would have gone into a WORM bucket
with a five-year retention lock, and neither could then be deleted.

**It produced false positives with real consequences.** The pattern `\bmay\b`
matches the month name. Any narrative referencing a date in May failed validation,
and `save_sar_report` raised — silently discarding a report for an alert that had
already been raised. Silently losing an alert draft also makes the prototype
impossible to audit.

**It was trivially satisfiable.** "Might indicate" fails; "indicates" passes. The
filter rewards deleting the qualifier while keeping the unsupported claim, which is
the opposite of what review should incentivise.

---

## What replaced it

`scripts/grounding.py` performs three checks and returns a structured
`GroundingReport` rather than a pass/fail verdict.

**1. Quantity traceability.** Every number in the narrative must appear in the
payload handed to the model, or be arithmetic derived from it. The allowlist is
built per-transaction and covers raw fields, model scores as probabilities and
percentages, the masked PAN suffix, legitimate derivations (amount ÷ card mean,
percentage above mean, z-score from amount/mean/std), and components of the
transaction timestamp — including 12-hour renderings, since 23:04 properly appears
as "11:04 PM". Matching uses a 1% relative tolerance. Statutory citations, list
numbering and window constants (24, 7, 5) are excluded as structure, not claims.

**2. Claims about unsupplied data.** Pattern checks for assertions the pipeline
cannot support at all: prior transactions, travel-time inferences, device or IP
telemetry, linked accounts, KYC records, case history, customer contact. These are
fabrications regardless of how confidently they are phrased.

**3. Advisory style note.** Vague hedging is still recorded, but it is an
observation, not grounds to reject a draft.

The system prompt was rewritten to match: it now forbids inventing figures,
forbids referencing unsupplied data, forbids cross-window comparisons, and
explicitly instructs the model to separate observation from inference and to avoid
overstating certainty.

Narratives that fail grounding are written to `compliance_logs/quarantine/` with the
verdict attached, then raise. Nothing is silently dropped. Saved drafts carry the
grounding verdict inline and remain unapproved and unsubmitted.

### Validation

The checker was tested against known-bad and known-good narratives rather than
assumed correct.

On the hallucinating narrative above it flags exactly three items — the invented
`67%`, the travel-time claim, and a fabricated reference to linked accounts — while
correctly accepting `150-fold` (7500 ÷ 50, a legitimate derivation).

On a real generated narrative it initially flagged six quantities, **all six of them
validator bugs**: the masked PAN suffix, a correct `1,442%` increase over the card
mean, ISO date fragments mis-parsed as negative numbers, and a 12-hour clock
rendering. Each was fixed. The narrative now reports 26 grounded quantities and zero
false positives.

## Known limits

- **Numeric grounding is necessary, not sufficient.** A narrative can use only real
  figures and still draw an unsupported inference from them. Detecting that needs
  claim-level entailment checking, which this does not attempt.
- **The unavailable-claims list is enumerated, not learned.** It catches the failure
  modes observed so far. A new phrasing will pass until the pattern is added.
- **Tolerance is a trade-off.** The 1% relative window accepts rounding; it would
  also accept a figure wrong by less than 1%.
- **Narrative evidence is not yet attached to the case ledger.** Phase 2 adds case
  assignment and disposition tracking, but draft files and quarantined narratives
  still live outside that event history. Durable evidence references, integrity
  proofs, retention, and recovery remain later work.

## Bearing on the wider system

The same principle applies to the alerts themselves. A model score is a ranking, not
a finding or an RGS determination. The threshold that converts it into an alert is a business decision about
how much investigator time a fraud is worth — which is why `scripts/evaluate.py`
reports alert volume and cost-weighted operating points rather than AUC alone.
